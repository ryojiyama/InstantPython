Probably, the Prime Minister and the civil official can also recognize that something is different. 文官が開きかけた口を閉じたのはその証拠だ。……宰相の視線がますます厳しくなったのも。

「三種類の暗号表が存在するという根拠は？」 The Prime Minister asked.

Let's say we encrypted three English short stories using three different codebooks. In the three types of texts, the most frequent characters will look different at first glance. だが、その割合はほぼ同じになるだろう。

If the percentages of the top three most frequent characters in a document are almost the same, and the types of characters are different, you can infer that there are multiple codebooks.

What Mia probably judged just by looking earlier was the pattern of the most frequent characters in that text. もちろん、一目でそんなことが出来るのはミーアのような特別な才能を持った人間だけだが、紙とペンがあれば誰でも可能な芸当だ。数えれば良いのだ。

「このように、この草の根には小さな粒が付いています。この粒が休耕地の回復に役に立つのです」 It refers to nitrogen fixation by root nodule bacteria. When looking at plants from the viewpoint of elements, carbon, oxygen, and hydrogen are overwhelmingly abundant. On the other hand, the three major elements of fertilizer are nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. Why does this difference occur?

Harvesting crops means taking away the elements that were there. If you grow wheat in Leiria, transport it to the royal capital, and consume it, you have taken hydrogen, oxygen, carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium from Leiria.

However, when a human eats wheat, hydrogen, oxygen, and carbon turn into water and carbon dioxide. In other words, they circulate through the air and rain in no time. To put it another way, even if taken, they return immediately. Carbohydrates made purely of water and carbon dioxide—that is, sugar—can actually be produced endlessly as long as there is light. Theoretically, continuous cropping problems would not occur with a crop that yields fruit made purely of sugar. もちろん、それだけを食べて人は生きていけないが。

However, nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium in the soil are different. Therefore, if you keep making crops, deficiencies arise. Especially nitrogen, essential for proteins and nucleic acids, is needed in the largest quantity among these three.

Ironically, nitrogen itself exists in massive quantities in the atmosphere. In the case of Earth, 78 percent of the air is nitrogen. By the way, carbon dioxide, the carbon source, is slightly less than 0.04 percent. Considering that Earth-origin organisms live essentially the same way in this world, it probably doesn't differ that much.

However, plants cannot directly utilize gaseous nitrogen molecules. It is the root nodule bacteria living symbiotically on legume roots that convert it into an accessible form. It is a chemical reaction that requires considerable energy even with the help of bacteria, but through symbiosis, it becomes worthwhile.

In other words, planting milk vetch kills two birds with one stone: producing nitrogen fertilizer for fallow land through roots while producing nectar through flowers. Of course, elements are taken away in the form of honey, but the quantity is negligible and mostly carbon and hydrogen.
